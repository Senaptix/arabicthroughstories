# Roots and word families

Answer to the Arabic teacher's point about verbs and forms.

**Recommendation: teach roots, not forms.** A back-of-book "word families"
appendix, built entirely from words the child has already met in the story.
Nothing is added to the story pages. Verb patterns (الأوزان I–X) stay out —
see the last section for the two exceptions.

## Why roots and not forms

Roots are the one insight that turns Arabic vocabulary from memorisation into
pattern recognition, and a child can _see_ it — three letters, a family of
words. Verb forms are a table to be learned, which is exactly the grammar
textbook this book is not.

The evidence is in the book's own vocabulary. Clustering all 285 glossary
words by root gives **~30 families covering ~85 words — about 30% of
everything taught.** Two of them carry a genuine "oh!" for a 9-year-old:

> **س ج د** — يَسْجُدُ _he prostrates_ (p4) · **الْمَسْجِدُ** _the masjid_ (p52)
> A masjid is the place where you do sujud. Same three letters.

> **س ل م** — سَلَامٌ _peace_ · سَالِمٌ _safe_ · **أَسْلَمَ** _he submitted_ · **الْمُسْلِمُونَ** _the Muslims_
> Islam, Muslim, salaam and safe are one family. The child works out what
> "Muslim" means from letters they already know.

Those two alone justify the appendix.

## How it is presented

Not as grammar. As **"words come in families."**

No terminology — no _root_, _triliteral_, _form_, _pattern_, _ﻭﺯﻥ_. Three
letters in a box, the family beneath, and the page where each was met, so the
child can flip back to the picture it belongs to.

The page numbers matter more than they look. They turn the appendix from a
list into a re-reading exercise, which is exactly what the teacher asked for.

## Where it goes

**Back of book, 2 pages.** Not in the glossary boxes — 200 of the 285 words
are only children (singletons), so a root printed next to each would be noise
on most pages and would clutter 50 boxes for the sake of 30 payoffs. And it
competes for the same margin space as the audio QR.

Two pages fits about **12 families** at readable size. Twelve that click beat
thirty that overwhelm. The full list below stays in this file as the working
record.

**Page-count consequence:** the appendix takes the book from 52 to 54 pages.
Worth confirming before it goes into the KDP setup.

## Verb forms — what to include

**No table of forms I–X.** For 7–12 that is a textbook, and it teaches shapes
the child has no words to hang on.

But two shape-changes occur in this book's own vocabulary and are worth three
lines each, phrased without any grammar term:

**Doubling the middle letter = making someone else do it**

- يَفْهَمُ _he understands_ (p9) → يُفَهِّمَ _to make understand_ (p32)

**Adding أ at the front = doing it to someone else**

- طَعَامٌ _food_ (p7) → يُطْعِمُنِي _He feeds me_ (p28)
- حَيٌّ _alive_ (p23) → يُحْيِي _He gives life_ (p30)

Both are concrete, both are already on the page, and neither requires the word
"form". Anything beyond these two waits for a later book.

## Series consequence

Like [vocabulary.md](vocabulary.md), this is a **series-level asset**. Book 2's
ع ب د entry should carry Book 1's words too, so the families visibly grow —
and the payoff moment (meeting مَكْسُور in Book 3 having learned كَسَرَ in
Book 1) only works if the index spans books. Entries will need a book number
as well as a page.

(It also fed the graded-vowelling plan — "familiar word", for the purpose of
dropping vowels, could reasonably have meant _familiar family_. That plan was
**scrapped on 2026-08-20** and every book now stays fully vowelled, so this
particular argument no longer applies. The cross-book index above stands on
its own regardless.)

**Book 2 gets this better than Book 1 can.** Its pages are not laid out yet, so
a small word-family box can sit on the page where the second member of a family
first appears — at the moment of recognition, which is where it teaches most.
Book 1 has to settle for the appendix, because retro-fitting boxes onto laid-out
pages costs more than it returns.
